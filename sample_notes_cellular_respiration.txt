Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose in the presence of oxygen to release energy stored in chemical bonds. The overall goal of cellular respiration is to produce ATP, the molecule that powers most cellular activities. Cellular respiration happens in three main stages: glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and the electron transport chain.

Glycolysis takes place in the cytoplasm and does not require oxygen. During glycolysis, one molecule of glucose is split into two molecules of pyruvate, producing a small amount of ATP and a molecule called NADH along the way. Because glycolysis does not need oxygen, it is considered an anaerobic process, and it occurs in both aerobic and anaerobic organisms.

If oxygen is available, pyruvate from glycolysis is transported into the mitochondria, where it is converted into a molecule called acetyl-CoA. Acetyl-CoA then enters the citric acid cycle, also known as the Krebs cycle, which takes place in the mitochondrial matrix. The citric acid cycle releases carbon dioxide as a waste product and generates additional NADH and FADH2, which carry high-energy electrons forward to the next stage.

The electron transport chain is a series of proteins embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH and FADH2 deliver their electrons to this chain, and as the electrons pass from protein to protein, energy is released and used to pump hydrogen ions across the membrane. This creates a concentration gradient that drives a protein called ATP synthase, which uses the flow of hydrogen ions back across the membrane to produce large amounts of ATP.

Oxygen plays a critical role at the end of the electron transport chain, where it acts as the final acceptor of the electrons, combining with hydrogen ions to form water. Without oxygen, the electron transport chain would stall, electrons would have nowhere to go, and ATP production would drop sharply. This is why cellular respiration is described as an aerobic process overall, even though its first stage, glycolysis, does not itself require oxygen.
